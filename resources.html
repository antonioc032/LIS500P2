Resource Guide

Gain a better understanding of the language used to talk about code and power

Internet-Based Technology (IBT): technology that uses many interconnected networks to provide a digital platform for communication through transferring data. Not everyone has equal access to IBT and there's still a lot of work to make important technology accessible to everyone around the world who needs them. Learn more by visiting this link: Digital Divides

Intersectionality: The idea that different parts of a person's upbringing and identity, such as race, gender, and class, can impact how they experience the world differently. While different demographic characteristics such as the ones we just mentioned play a big part in intersectionality, another huge part of it is their own past experiences. Although some people with similar backgrounds can have similar perceptions of things in the world, it ultimately comes down to their own unique experiences that build up their ideas and opinions. Lived experiences are arguably one of the biggest factors that go into how different people perceive the world and how they experience intersectionality. You can learn a lot more about intersectionality in depth by visiting this link: Intersectionality 101

Implicit Bias: The preconcieved beliefs that people have that may not be recognized. The beliefs are usually assumptions about different groups of people. Implicit bias in some cases can be built up through generations of stereotypical representation in media. For example, in the Implicit Association test I link below, there's a test for gender implicit association that is supposed to test your implicit biases on fields in science vs liberal arts against the words "male" or "female". One thing we realized after taking or learning about this test is that specifically female representation in science media, such as movies, is something that is lacking, especially decades ago but still today. Under representation such as that is something that can build up implicit biases over time. You can take the Implicit Association Tests here and learn more about your own implicit biases by visiting this link, but don't think of it as a clear description of your non-concious thoughts, it's just to get you thinking: Harvard IAT
