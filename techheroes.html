Our Tech Heroes

Meet the people who inspire us through their work in technology, diversity, and inclusion.

Dr. Allison Scott

Dr. Allison Scott is definitely someone worth learning about. She is the CEO of the Kapor Foundation, which focuses on giving access and opportunities to those who need them. Her work focuses on making technology fields more equitable and addressing racial and economic inequality. She has also worked to make computer science education more accessible, especially for women of color.

Dr. Joy Buolamwini

Dr. Joy Buolamwini is another inspiring figure in the technology industry. She is the founder of the Algorithmic Justice League, an organization that focuses on identifying and addressing bias in artificial intelligence. Through her research, she discovered that some facial recognition systems were less accurate when identifying women with darker skin tones. Her work demonstrates how race and gender can intersect to create inequality in technology.

Learn More:

  • Algorithmic Justice League
  • Joy Buolamwini - MIT

Ayah Bdeir

Ayah Bdeir is an engineer and the founder of littleBits, a collection of electronic building blocks that connect with magnets. These blocks let people experiment with electronics and build projects without having to start with complicated circuits. Her work makes creating with technology more approachable for beginners.

Bdeir's work connects to our class because it challenges assumptions about who can be an inventor. Implicit bias can affect who gets encouraged to explore engineering. Looking at access through intersectionality also means asking how barriers overlap: a student facing gender stereotypes may also attend a school that cannot afford equipment. Making tools easier to use helps, but access to those tools and support from teachers still matter.
